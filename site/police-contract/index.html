About Nick Mastrogiorgio

Public Servant. Community Advocate. Retired Police Lieutenant. Entrepreneur. Independent Voice.

Nicholas “Nick” Mastrogiorgio is a lifelong Mount Vernon resident, fourth-generation Mount Vernon native, retired police lieutenant, former police union president, entrepreneur, technology professional and outspoken advocate for government transparency, accountability and responsible leadership.

His roots in Mount Vernon run more than a century deep. His great-grandfather built the family's home on Terrace Avenue, and generations of the Mastrogiorgio family have served the city through law enforcement, public works, education and municipal government. Public service is more than a career choice for Nick; it is a family tradition.

From Finance to Public Service

Before beginning his career in law enforcement, Nick worked in banking and the financial services industry at Citibank, where he served as a CitiBusiness analyst and worked with small business owners. His early professional experience in banking and the Wall Street financial services industry included holding Series 7, Series 63, and Life and Health Insurance licenses.

That experience provided him with a strong foundation in finance, business operations, financial analysis and fiscal responsibility — skills that would later influence his approach to municipal government, labor negotiations and public policy.

In 2002, Nick followed in his father's footsteps and joined the Mount Vernon Police Department, beginning a law enforcement career that would span more than two decades.

A Career in Law Enforcement

Throughout his career with the Mount Vernon Police Department, Nick served as a police officer, detective, sergeant and lieutenant. He spent approximately a decade as a detective, developing extensive experience in criminal investigations, evidence collection, investigative techniques and complex problem-solving.

As he advanced through the ranks, Nick assumed increasingly significant leadership and administrative responsibilities, including assignments within the Patrol Division and Support Services Division.

His background in information technology also allowed him to contribute to the department beyond traditional policing. He became involved in police technology, information systems, operational modernization and the integration of technology into law enforcement operations.

After more than 22 years of service, Nick retired from the Mount Vernon Police Department in 2024 with the rank of lieutenant.

Fighting for the Mount Vernon Police Department

Among Nick's most significant roles was serving as President of the Mount Vernon Police Association, the labor organization representing the sworn members of the Mount Vernon Police Department.

As PBA President, Nick became an outspoken advocate for the men and women of the department, fighting for fair compensation, improved working conditions, adequate staffing, officer safety and the resources necessary to effectively protect the residents of Mount Vernon.

During a period marked by staffing shortages, the departure of experienced police officers, difficult contract negotiations and growing concerns about public safety, Nick regularly challenged city officials to address the underlying problems facing the department.

He brought these issues into the public spotlight through media interviews, public statements, community meetings and social media, often questioning the priorities and financial decisions of city government.

His advocacy was never simply about police salaries or union contracts. It was about the long-term stability of the department and the quality of public safety services available to the people of Mount Vernon.

Nick also worked to strengthen relationships between law enforcement and the community, engaging with community leaders, clergy and advocacy organizations. He believes that supporting police officers and demanding accountability within law enforcement are not mutually exclusive, and that effective policing requires trust, cooperation and mutual respect between officers and the communities they serve.

An Independent Voice for Mount Vernon

Nick's advocacy has extended well beyond the walls of the police department.

As a lifelong resident, he has consistently spoken out on issues affecting Mount Vernon's taxpayers, homeowners, businesses, municipal employees and families.

He has been particularly vocal about government transparency, fiscal mismanagement, rising property taxes, political favoritism, infrastructure deterioration, economic development and the need for elected officials to be held accountable for their decisions.

He has questioned tax incentive agreements that benefit developers while potentially shifting financial burdens onto existing taxpayers, criticized decisions to increase elected officials' compensation while essential city services struggled, and advocated for meaningful investments in public safety, infrastructure and youth programs.

Nick believes that loyalty to a city does not mean remaining silent about its problems. Rather, it means caring enough about that city to confront those problems, ask difficult questions and demand better from its leadership.

In 2025, following his retirement from law enforcement, Nick ran for the Mount Vernon City Council in the Democratic primary. His campaign focused on transparency, accountability, fiscal responsibility, public safety and returning government to the people it serves.

Although he fell short of winning a seat, his commitment to Mount Vernon and its residents did not end on Election Day.

His belief remains the same: elected officials work for the people, public office is a responsibility rather than a privilege, and no individual or political organization should be above public scrutiny.

Technology, Business and Entrepreneurship

In addition to his career in public service, Nick is the founder and owner of MastroTek, a technology and digital marketing company dedicated to helping small businesses succeed.

With an extensive background in information technology, Nick has worked in computer systems, networking, technical support, website development, search engine optimization, cybersecurity, digital marketing and technology consulting.

Through MastroTek, he has helped businesses establish their online presence, improve their visibility, modernize their technology, attract customers and navigate an increasingly digital business environment.

His professional experience also extends into private security, security management, digital investigations, electronic evidence collection and data recovery.

Nick's combined experience in finance, law enforcement, investigations, technology, business and management provides him with a unique perspective on the challenges facing local government, public institutions and the private sector.

He is a strong advocate for using technology to improve government efficiency, reduce waste, increase transparency and make information more accessible to the public.

The Purpose of NickMastro.com

NickMastro.com was created to provide an independent platform for news, commentary, analysis, opinion and discussion surrounding the issues that matter.

While Mount Vernon remains at the heart of Nick's advocacy, the website also explores broader issues involving government, politics, law enforcement, public safety, technology, business and society.

Drawing upon decades of professional experience and a lifetime of community involvement, Nick seeks to examine issues beyond the headlines, question conventional narratives, encourage informed discussion and bring attention to matters that might otherwise be overlooked.

The goal is not to defend a political party, protect elected officials or promote a particular political establishment. It is to examine the facts, ask the questions that need to be asked and encourage greater accountability from those entrusted with public responsibility.

Nick believes that disagreement is healthy, informed debate is necessary, and silence in the face of wrongdoing accomplishes nothing.

He does not expect everyone to agree with his opinions, but he believes everyone deserves access to the information necessary to form their own.

Public service does not end when you retire, take off the uniform or lose an election. It continues for as long as you are willing to stand up, speak out and fight for the community you call home.
